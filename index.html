Does delivery punctuality relate to review satisfaction?

Observed historical association, not proof that delays alone cause lower scores.

HOW WE VALIDATED IT

From raw orders to evidence.

01Define lateness carefully

Compare delivery calendar dates against promised dates to avoid time-of-day misclassification.

02Compare rated groups

89,443 on-time vs 6,381 late rated deliveries; nonparametric Mann–Whitney U.

03Bootstrap confidence

Estimate a 95% interval for the difference in mean review scores.

04Check robustness

After excluding deliveries beyond 60 days, the difference remained 2.0118 points (n = 95,533).

RESEARCH INTERPRETATION

Late delivery and lower ratings are strongly associated in this sample.

Other influences (product quality, seller behaviour, expectations) may also affect ratings. Historical observational data cannot establish a causal effect on its own.

DATA PROVENANCE

The numbers have a source.

This website displays verified historical findings recovered from the RetailIQ project’s SQL exports, analysis logs and model evaluation outputs. It is not connected to a live database in this static build.

Different analytic questions use different valid subsets. For instance, the delivery operations analysis contains 96,470 valid records, the rating study includes 95,824 reviewed deliveries, and the segmentation study profiles 93,358 unique customers.

Monthly and state late-delivery visualizations use the first timestamp-based exploration. The final research classification uses calendar dates, changing the total late rate from 8.11% to 6.77%.
